# Final consolidation: three five-times-hard chat-development-system plans

> The final adoption decision selects Evidence Forge. Its frozen scope and proof
> protocol are defined in `21-selected-evidence-forge.md`.

## 1. Decision

The five deepened directions are consolidated into three buildable challenge
plans:

1. **Evidence Forge** — adaptive multi-agent development plus owned Git delivery;
2. **Quiet Room** — chat-native shared development truth plus low-interruption
   autonomy;
3. **Learning Forge** — evidence-backed project-local orchestration that improves
   across repeated campaigns.

All three retain a minimal native chat, provider, persistence, authority, and Git
substrate. They differ in the quality frontier that should consume the remaining
B60–B180 development time.

## 2. Why five did not remain independent

### RoomOS and Quiet Autopilot are one human problem

An ambient, low-interruption partner cannot work without a trustworthy model of
goals, assumptions, decisions, tasks, and artifacts. Quiet Autopilot without
RoomOS either asks from hidden state or acts without an understandable control
surface. They therefore combine into Quiet Room.

### ForgeLab and Shipyard are one delivery problem

Candidate competition, debate, and verification have no development value until
the selected work becomes a clean accepted commit. Shipyard without ForgeLab can
be a robust serial wrapper; ForgeLab without Shipyard is coordination theatre.
They therefore combine into Evidence Forge.

### Project Brain needs a real forge beneath it

Learning cannot be judged from stored chat or one episode. It requires repeated
Evidence Forge-like campaigns, automatic evidence, matched later outcomes,
negative transfer, and forgetting. Learning Forge remains separate because its
time horizon and evaluator are qualitatively different.

## 3. Common non-negotiable substrate

Every plan must cross the same floor:

- native local chat-like control surface;
- broad goal and versioned campaign identity;
- Codex, Claude, and Grok adapter capability/readiness contracts;
- persisted messages, runs, goals, artifacts, and terminal states;
- workspace authority and initial dirty-state snapshot;
- controller-owned process identity, cancellation, and bounded output;
- truthful command/test/artifact evidence;
- one provider-backed development task ending in a clean accepted commit;
- restart without fabricated or lost terminal truth;
- compact human-readable current state and residual unknowns.

This floor is deliberately stronger than DevRelay v1 but should still be
reachable around B30. It receives no “100/100” completion score.

## 4. Shared five-times proof contract

A plan is not called five-times harder until matched calibration shows:

- B30 crosses the common floor but leaves at least three plan-specific mechanism
  groups weak;
- B60 improves a downstream artifact, not only protocol coverage;
- B120 improves confirmation or heldout outcomes over B60;
- useful scored work remains at B120, with B180 as the full target;
- direct forwarding and UI-rich forwarding remain materially below a strong
  reference;
- the plan-specific mutant fails for the intended causal reason;
- multi-agent evidence is executable and repays coordination cost;
- one pre-registered V2 product change distinguishes durable architecture;
- P0 conformance does not determine ranking;
- run-to-run provider variance does not overwhelm the result.

# Plan 1 — Evidence Forge

## 5. Product thesis

Build a chat-native development forge that chooses useful collaboration methods,
creates independent executable evidence, compares candidates, verifies risky
work, and owns delivery into a clean accepted Git result. It optimizes accepted
software quality and critical path rather than agent count.

This plan absorbs **ForgeLab ×5** and **Shipyard ×5**.

## 6. Public objective shape

> Build the strongest local Git-native development room you can. A human states
> a broad goal in chat; the room should determine when sequential work,
> consultation, independent verification, or competing implementations will
> improve the result. It must deliver tested, reviewable Git outcomes, preserve
> user work, remain truthful under provider/process failure, and expose only the
> progress and decisions that matter.

The public brief names outcomes and available providers, not role counts, debate
rounds, task-graph classes, worktree layouts, or a required orchestration recipe.

## 7. Seven load-bearing mechanisms

### 7.1 Adaptive collaboration choice

The same frozen product is evaluated on tightly coupled, parallel-ready,
architecture-ambiguous, verifier-sensitive, and continuation tasks. A single
method should not dominate all five.

### 7.2 Executable disagreement

Competing technical claims must become frozen prototypes, tests, measurements,
or reproductions. Consensus prose has no independent value.

### 7.3 Candidate portfolio

Candidates begin from the same digest, represent materially different
assumptions, and run under a shared comparison contract. Useful evidence from
losers may be retained.

### 7.4 Owned integration

The controller—not a provider summary—owns diff review, staging, acceptance,
commit, and rerun on the integrated tree. A provider may be unable to write
`.git` and still contribute a valid candidate.

### 7.5 Dirty-state and stale-base safety

User changes, generated files, overlapping candidates, a moving base, and late
provider success are composed in heldout episodes.

### 7.6 Maker/verifier closure

A fresh verifier can reopen completion only with a reproducible finding against
a frozen artifact. Repair and exact closure precede final acceptance.

### 7.7 Provider/process degradation

Provider unavailability, malformed output, timeout, orphan descendants,
cancellation, and restart trigger bounded replanning without silently weakening
the objective.

## 8. Inner development portfolio

At least five small-to-medium Git projects are required:

1. **Tight seam:** parallel writers increase rework;
2. **Parallel modules:** independent implementation can shorten critical path;
3. **Architecture fork:** two viable designs require an experiment;
4. **High-loss defect:** maker tests miss a reproducible hidden failure;
5. **Continuation:** a later requirement stresses the chosen architecture.

Project topics and identifiers change between public, confirmation, and heldout
populations, while the declared need remains stable.

## 9. Quality vector

- accepted functional milestones;
- hidden/confirmation defect escape;
- time-to-first-valid and time-to-best-integrated;
- unique decision-changing evidence;
- candidate family diversity and selection regret;
- duplicate work, conflict, and integration tail;
- preserved user-owned changes;
- exact command/test/commit evidence;
- process/provider recovery and partial-value salvage;
- V2 change time, regression, and change amplification.

Hard gates include lost user work, authority escape, fabricated success,
unreproducible terminal claims, and accepting a stale or unintegrated artifact.

## 10. Mutants that must separate

- direct single-Codex forwarding;
- rich chat that still forwards prompts;
- fixed sequential planner;
- indiscriminate parallel planner;
- debate/review theatre without reproduced evidence;
- maker-only completion;
- unsafe provider-owned Git integration;
- robust but slow serial reference.

The robust serial reference should remain on a Pareto frontier; speed is not
allowed to erase correctness.

## 11. B30/B60/B120/B180 curve

- **B30:** common floor and conservative single-provider delivery;
- **B60:** artifact/task ownership, verification, controller-owned integration;
- **B120:** adaptive methods, candidate experiments, failure recovery across the
  five-project portfolio;
- **B180:** routing refinement, dogfood-driven UX, V2 continuation, assurance
  cost control.

## 12. Five-times rejection gate

Reject this plan if forwarding achieves more than roughly 70% of the strong
reference's semantic milestones, if only one inner project affects ranking, or
if extra agents mostly increase messages and review cost.

## 13. Why it is the leading plan

It directly tests the original hypothesis: richer relations among agents can
improve software development. It also resolves the current concrete Git
integration/evidence gap. Its main cost is building and calibrating a portfolio
of inner projects.

# Plan 2 — Quiet Room

## 14. Product thesis

Build a chat-native shared development room that keeps goals, assumptions,
decisions, tasks, and artifacts coherent while making safe progress through long
human silence. It optimizes accepted development and scarce human attention
together.

This plan absorbs **RoomOS ×5** and **Quiet Autopilot ×5**, while retaining only
the minimal Shipyard delivery floor.

## 15. Public objective shape

> Build a local development partner that a human can guide through natural chat
> without becoming its project manager. The room should preserve shared truth,
> make reversible progress autonomously, surface only high-value intervention,
> and remain understandable after absence, correction, conflict, and restart.

## 16. Seven load-bearing mechanisms

### 16.1 Causal correction

Correcting or retracting a message invalidates derived assumptions/tasks without
rewriting history or discarding still-useful artifacts.

### 16.2 Semantic branch and merge

Alternative investigations branch from one goal and later merge selected
decisions, tests, and dissent into current room truth.

### 16.3 Stale concurrent users and agents

Task claims, decision acceptance, message correction, and artifact updates
overlap and converge with actionable conflict feedback.

### 16.4 Context compaction

Long rooms fit bounded active context while fresh agents and returning humans can
still recover decisive requirements, provenance, and unresolved cruxes.

### 16.5 Value-of-intervention policy

Uncertainty is classified by loss, reversibility, evidence, critical path, and
deadline. The evaluator supplies long silence and one scarce reply opportunity.

### 16.6 Finite proactive work

Background jobs have identity, scope, dedupe, budget, stop condition, next
eligibility, and measured downstream yield. Low-yield recurrence is suspended.

### 16.7 Cross-layer crash reconciliation

Room events, actual Git artifacts, active processes, and client projections are
reconciled after crashes at opposing persistence boundaries.

## 17. Human-availability episodes

1. normal interactive development;
2. 90-minute silence after the broad goal;
3. exactly one permitted human answer;
4. human priority change during integration;
5. return after compaction and restart;
6. scheduled maintenance colliding with foreground work.

The human never fills out routine quality forms. Intervention value is computed
from the consequences of answered, unanswered, and avoided decisions.

## 18. Quality vector

- accepted milestones per wall hour;
- human interruptions and estimated information value;
- blocked time caused by routine unanswered questions;
- unsafe assumptions and downstream rework;
- progress during absence;
- correction invalidation precision;
- stale/contradictory room projections;
- return-to-correct-understanding task time;
- notification precision/recall;
- scheduled-job yield, collision, and provider use;
- restart/compaction equivalence;
- V2 change cost for cross-room or remote-observer support.

## 19. Mutants that must separate

- passive mention-only chat;
- silent autonomy that never asks;
- approval-heavy safe system;
- typed-card UI with no causal propagation;
- transcript summarizer that loses source anchors;
- always-on scheduled agents;
- volatile room state;
- notification-rich but low-yield dashboard.

## 20. B30/B60/B120/B180 curve

- **B30:** common floor, assumptions, simple current-state projection;
- **B60:** correction propagation, ambient intervention queue, safe autonomy;
- **B120:** branch/merge, compaction, scheduled-job controls, silent-human
  episodes;
- **B180:** risk inversion, crash reconciliation, user-task refinement, V2
  evolution.

## 21. Five-times rejection gate

Reject if plain chat search matches RoomOS user tasks, if fewer questions are
achieved through hidden unsafe action, or if background work fails to produce
accepted artifacts.

## 22. Why it is second

Quiet Room best matches the desired everyday experience: coding stays primary
while the room adds useful intelligence. Its main risk is evaluator subjectivity,
so automatic downstream outcomes and task-based human simulations must remain
stronger than visual-polish scoring.

# Plan 3 — Learning Forge

## 23. Product thesis

Build a development room that delivers current work and learns bounded,
project-local orchestration knowledge that improves later matched work. It must
separate reusable evidence from episode noise, detect negative transfer and
provider drift, and forget stale or contaminated guidance.

This plan retains **Project Brain ×5** and embeds a constrained Evidence Forge
substrate. It is not a memory UI.

## 24. Public objective shape

> Build a local development studio that becomes more effective as it works on a
> project. Learn only from automatic evidence, keep guidance conditional and
> auditable, validate it against later outcomes, and safely revise or forget it
> when project or provider conditions change.

## 25. Seven load-bearing mechanisms

### 25.1 Automatic episode evidence

Task identity, context, provider/version/effort, duration, decision, artifact,
test, review, integration, and outcome links are captured without human forms.

### 25.2 Conditional priors

Duration, routing, parallelism, review, and experiment priors include scope,
sample count, uncertainty, contrary cases, and validation version.

### 25.3 Shadow promotion

New guidance first predicts or recommends without controlling work. A matched
later outcome must justify promotion.

### 25.4 Method-card/skill synthesis

Repeated successful evidence may become a compact conditional method card or
skill. Raw transcripts and benchmark answers may not be copied as guidance.

### 25.5 Negative-transfer defense

Superficially similar subsystems require different methods. The system must
detect harm, narrow scope, lower confidence, and roll back guidance.

### 25.6 Provider and project drift

Provider versions/readiness, architecture, workload, or team preference changes
invalidate old priors and trigger bounded re-exploration.

### 25.7 Contamination, expiry, and forgetting

Public evaluator feedback, replaced commits, obsolete decisions, and bloated
context are quarantined, expired, compacted, or forgotten with auditable reason.

## 26. Repeated campaign population

Learning Forge requires a sequence rather than one project:

1. two baseline episodes establishing evidence;
2. one matched repeat where guidance should help;
3. one similar-looking negative-transfer episode;
4. one provider/version drift episode;
5. one contaminated-memory episode;
6. one V2 project change where prior architecture knowledge may help or hurt.

All episodes use frozen bases and automatic evaluators. Counterfactual/no-memory
runs are required for claims of learning benefit.

## 27. Quality vector

- accepted later-campaign improvement over no-memory baseline;
- time-to-first-valid and integration quality;
- duration/routing regret and calibration;
- method-card adoption linked to artifact change;
- negative-transfer rate and recovery delay;
- provider/project drift detection;
- stale-context defect count;
- memory/context size and retrieval overhead;
- contamination detection;
- expiry/rollback correctness;
- human correction needed to undo bad learning.

## 28. Mutants that must separate

- transcript retrieval presented as learning;
- global preset learned from one success;
- duration-only router;
- no-forgetting memory store;
- benchmark-answer memorizer;
- confident stale provider routing;
- shadow policy that never changes work;
- complex learning layer with no matched outcome gain.

## 29. B30/B60/B120/B180 curve

- **B30:** common substrate and evidence capture;
- **B60:** scoped priors and shadow predictions;
- **B120:** matched promotion, method card, negative-transfer handling;
- **B180:** drift, contamination, forgetting, V2 adaptation and ablation.

This time curve refers to outer product development. Evaluating the frozen
artifact still requires repeated inner episodes, but each is bounded.

## 30. Five-times rejection gate

Reject if memory receives credit without a matched later improvement, if raw
transcript retrieval explains most gains, or if evaluation cost/noise makes the
counterfactual unreliable.

## 31. Why it is third

Learning Forge has the highest theoretical ceiling but the highest proof cost.
It should not be built first: reliable delivery evidence and repeated campaigns
must exist before learning is meaningful.

## 32. Pairwise decision

### Evidence Forge versus Quiet Room

- Choose Evidence Forge to test whether multi-agent relations improve technical
  artifacts.
- Choose Quiet Room to optimize the daily human experience and interruption.
- Evidence Forge has a more objective evaluator; Quiet Room has broader product
  UX value.

### Evidence Forge versus Learning Forge

- Evidence Forge can show value in one portfolio run.
- Learning Forge requires repeated matched episodes and counterfactuals.
- Learning Forge should reuse Evidence Forge evidence rather than replace it.

### Quiet Room versus Learning Forge

- Quiet Room adapts within one campaign from current risk.
- Learning Forge adapts across campaigns from historical evidence.
- Both can fail through hidden automation, but Learning Forge has greater
  contamination and negative-transfer risk.

## 33. Recommended choice

Build **Evidence Forge** first.

Reasons:

1. It directly tests the user's central claim that richer agent relationships
   should improve software development.
2. It has objective downstream Git/test/artifact evidence.
3. It fixes the already observed provider-commit and terminal-evidence gaps.
4. It can distinguish useful consultation, candidate competition, verification,
   and parallel implementation in one portfolio.
5. Its mechanisms create a natural B30/B60/B120/B180 frontier.

Pre-register **Quiet Room** as the V2 change axis: after Evidence Forge V1 is
frozen, require comparable development under long human silence and one scarce
intervention opportunity.

Defer **Learning Forge** until repeated trustworthy episode evidence exists. It
then becomes a separate longitudinal benchmark rather than speculative V1
architecture.

## 34. Next proof step

Do not build the full product immediately. First build the Evidence Forge
evaluator skeleton and three discriminating inner episodes:

1. tightly coupled task where indiscriminate parallelism loses;
2. architecture task where candidate experiments can win;
3. verifier-sensitive task with one high-loss hidden mutant.

Then run direct forwarding, rich forwarding, serial reference, and adaptive
reference. Only if their failure profiles separate should the remaining two
episodes and B30/B60/B120 live calibration be funded.
